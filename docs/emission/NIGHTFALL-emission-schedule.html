Why 89,999,999.25 and not 90,000,000

The reward is a right shift on an integer number of darks, and reward >> halvings truncates. From era 10 onward the halved value is no longer exactly representable, so a fraction of a dark is dropped every block and never minted.

The cap is therefore an upper bound the curve approaches and never touches. It is also enforced separately: the payable reward is clamped so the cap cannot be breached even by one dark, whatever the schedule says.

Half of all NIGHT in the first era

6 × 7,500,000 = 45,000,000, exactly half the cap — that identity is asserted by a test, so the constants cannot drift apart unnoticed.

75 % is minted by the end of era 1 and 99 % during era 6. The remaining 1 % takes another eighty years. 89,000,000 NIGHT is reached at block 49,333,334, about 23.4 years in.

Fees are burned, not paid

While the subsidy is positive the fee is destroyed and the miner receives only the subsidy — so circulating supply is always below the minted figures in this table by the total burned.

Once the subsidy reaches zero the coinbase kernel carries the block's fees instead: nothing is minted, nothing is burned, and the miner is paid. That is the whole long-term security budget. There is no tail emission.

The dates are projections, not promises

Every date assumes the 15-second target holds on average from genesis (16 August 2026). Difficulty retargets every block over a 90-block weighted average, so the network tracks that target closely — but a decade of drift is still a decade of drift.

Block heights and reward amounts, by contrast, are exact. They are consensus, not forecast.
